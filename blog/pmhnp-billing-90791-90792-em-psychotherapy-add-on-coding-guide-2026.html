PMHNP Billing: 90791 vs 90792 and E/M + Psychotherapy Add-On Coding Guide (2026)

Small CPT selection errors create expensive denial loops. This guide helps PMHNP teams standardize psychiatric intake and follow-up coding so claims clear on first pass.

By PMHNP Billing editorial team · Published March 1, 2026 · 10 min read · Updated July 9, 2026

Psychiatric claims can look technically complete and still deny because of one code-pairing or documentation mismatch. For PMHNP practices, the most frequent risk zone is the handoff between diagnostic evaluations (90791/90792) and ongoing E/M + psychotherapy add-on coding.

This national guide gives billing and clinical teams a practical framework to choose code paths consistently, document what payers expect, and reduce rework. For medication workflows that trigger extra utilization controls, pair this with the prior authorization guide for PMHNPs.

Quick takeaway: Standardized code-selection rules plus same-day chart QA can prevent a large share of avoidable psychiatric coding denials before claims are submitted.

90791 vs 90792: practical differentiation checklist

Use 90791 when:

  • The encounter is a psychiatric diagnostic evaluation without separately billable medical services.
  • Documentation centers on psychiatric history, symptom profile, risk, diagnosis, and care planning.
  • No medication-management decisioning is documented as a billable medical service component.

Use 90792 when:

  • The psychiatric diagnostic evaluation includes medical services relevant to diagnosis/treatment.
  • The note supports medical assessment/management content expected by payer policy.
  • The encounter clearly captures medical complexity and treatment implications, not just psych history.
Compliance note: Code definitions and payer edits can vary by plan and contract. Always validate against your payer policy and current CPT guidance before finalizing internal rules.

Same-day psychiatric coding QA checklist

  • Confirm visit intent first: diagnostic evaluation vs ongoing management.
  • Validate whether medical services were actually performed/documented for initial evaluations.
  • For combined encounters, ensure E/M and psychotherapy sections are clearly distinguishable.
  • Check payer rules for code pairing, telehealth modifiers, and place-of-service edits.
  • Verify diagnosis pointers and problem linkage are consistent across note and claim.
  • Run pre-submit denial edits by payer before claim release.

30-day implementation plan for PMHNP practices

Week 1: Baseline your failure modes

Review the last 60 days of psychiatric denials and tag each to a code-family error, documentation gap, or payer-edit conflict.

Week 2: Standardize clinical templates

Deploy chart templates that force clear separation of evaluation, medical management, and psychotherapy elements.

Week 3: Build payer coding matrix discipline

Create one source of truth per payer for allowed pairings, POS/modifier logic, and known psychiatric edit quirks.

Week 4: Run closed-loop QA

Audit first-pass acceptance and denial-rework turnaround. Feed lessons back into templates and claim-scrubber rules.

FAQ

Can we bill psychotherapy add-ons every follow-up visit?

Only when documentation supports both services and payer rules allow the pairing for that scenario. Build rule checks by payer to avoid assumptions.

What should billing teams review before release?

Visit intent, code-family selection, documentation sufficiency, and payer-specific edits. A two-minute pre-submit checklist often prevents multi-week rework.

What KPI should we watch first?

Start with first-pass acceptance rate for psychiatric code families and denial overturn cycle time for coding-related denials.
