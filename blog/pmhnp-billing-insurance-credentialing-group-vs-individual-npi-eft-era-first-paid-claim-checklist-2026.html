What “Credentialed” Really Means in Revenue Terms

Practices often say, “We are credentialed,” when only one piece is complete. Revenue-ready status usually requires all three layers below:

  1. Provider credentialing approval (individual PMHNP accepted for participation).
  2. Billing enrollment alignment (correct billing/rendering setup by entity and payer).
  3. Payment enrollment activation (ERA + EFT configured so remits and funds actually flow).

If any layer is missing, claims may adjudicate incorrectly, pend, or pay out-of-network rates.

Group vs Individual NPI: The Decision Path That Prevents Rework

Individual NPI (Type 1)

  • Identifies the rendering PMHNP.
  • Must align with licensing, taxonomy, and payer enrollment records.
  • Usually required for credentialing and rendering claim lines.

Organization NPI (Type 2)

  • Represents the billing entity (group or practice).
  • Often required when claims are submitted under the group TIN.
  • Critical for payment routing, EFT enrollment, and ERA setup.
Fast rule: if the practice will bill under a group/entity TIN, design the enrollment workflow around a Type 2 billing path early, not as a post-approval fix.

First-Paid-Claim Checklist (Use Before Go-Live)

  • Rendering PMHNP is active in payer roster with matching Type 1 NPI + taxonomy.
  • Billing entity Type 2 enrollment is active where group billing applies.
  • Contract effective dates loaded into PM + clearinghouse systems.
  • EFT enrollment is active and bank details match payer records exactly.
  • ERA enrollment is active and routed to the correct reconciliation endpoint.
  • Claim scrubber has payer-specific edits for psychiatric services and telehealth modifiers/POS where relevant.
  • Denial queue has named owner, daily cadence, and 24-hour triage SLA.

The 5 Most Common Post-Credentialing Revenue Failures

  1. Effective date mismatch: claim date predates payer activation date.
  2. Rendering/billing mismatch: Type 1 and Type 2 paths are reversed or incomplete.
  3. Taxonomy drift: CAQH, NPPES, and payer records are out of sync.
  4. EFT not active: claims adjudicate but checks/remits route incorrectly or delay.
  5. No reconciliation owner: ERA files arrive but are not posted rapidly, hiding underpayments and denials.

KPIs to Track in the First 60 Days

  • Days from submission to credentialing approval (by payer)
  • Days from approval to first clean claim acceptance
  • First-pass acceptance rate for new payer enrollments
  • Percent of claims denied for enrollment/eligibility/provider setup reasons
  • Days from adjudication to posted payment (EFT + ERA performance)
