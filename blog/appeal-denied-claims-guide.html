How to Appeal Denied Insurance Claims: A Step-by-Step Guide for PMHNPs

Claim denials are a frustrating reality for psychiatric mental health nurse practitioners. The good news: 60-70% of denied claims can be overturned on appeal when handled correctly.

Common Reasons Claims Are Denied

  • Missing or incorrect prior authorization
  • Coding errors (wrong CPT or diagnosis codes)
  • Lack of medical necessity documentation
  • Patient eligibility issues
  • Timely filing deadlines missed

The Appeal Process: Step by Step

Step 1: Review the Denial Letter

Every denial letter contains crucial information: the specific reason for denial, the appeal deadline (usually 180 days), and instructions for submitting your appeal.

Step 2: Gather Supporting Documentation

  • Original claim and denial letter
  • Clinical notes from the date of service
  • Treatment plan showing medical necessity
  • Any prior authorizations obtained
  • Patient's insurance card and eligibility verification

Step 3: Write an Effective Appeal Letter

Your appeal letter should:

  • Reference the specific claim number and patient information
  • Address the exact reason for denial
  • Provide clinical justification for medical necessity
  • Include supporting documentation
  • Be professional and concise

When to Escalate

If your first-level appeal is denied, you have options:

  • Second-level appeal: Request a medical director review
  • External review: Independent third-party review
  • State insurance commissioner: For persistent issues

Prevention: Reducing Future Denials

  • Verify benefits and eligibility before every visit
  • Obtain prior authorizations when required
  • Use correct CPT codes with appropriate modifiers
  • Document medical necessity thoroughly
  • Submit claims within timely filing limits
