PMHNP Billing: CAQH Re-Attestation and Re-Credentialing Checklist (2026)

A practical operations guide to stop credentialing drift before it turns into payer claim holds and delayed cash flow.

By PMHNP Billing editorial team · Published March 3, 2026 · Updated July 9, 2026

Initial credentialing is only one part of enrollment operations. A missed CAQH re-attestation, payer recredentialing request, or demographic update can contribute to payer information requests or claim holds.

This checklist is built for owner-operators and practice managers who already have active panels and want a more reliable maintenance process.

Primary source: Use CAQH ProView for the current profile and re-attestation process, then verify each payer's separate recredentialing requests and deadlines.

CAQH and recredentialing control calendar

Use a single credentialing control calendar with named owners and due dates. This prevents payer-specific notices from living in individual inboxes.

CAQH Re-Attestation Checklist (Run Every Cycle)

  1. Confirm legal name, TIN, and service address formatting are identical across systems.
  2. Validate professional license and malpractice expiration dates are current.
  3. Review hospital affiliation and supervising/collaborative fields (if required by payer).
  4. Reconcile all document uploads with payer-requested file types and date windows.
  5. Complete attestation and export confirmation with timestamp.
  6. Notify billing operations that credentialing status was renewed.
Execution tip: Follow CAQH completion with a documented payer-status check. Attestation alone does not prove that a separate payer file has been updated.

Top Claim-Hold Triggers After Re-Credentialing

  • Profile mismatch: Service location or taxonomy differs between CAQH and payer file.
  • Incomplete packet closure: Payer marked file “in progress” after requesting one missing document.
  • Stale rendering record: Group and rendering links not fully updated after ownership/admin changes.
  • ERA/EFT drift: Enrollment maintenance was not aligned with credentialing updates.

Stabilization workflow if holds begin

1. Triage and contain variance

  • Tag denials/pends by root cause: enrollment, coding, auth, or medical necessity.
  • Create payer-specific work queues so enrollment fixes do not get mixed with appeal work.

2. Repair payer files

  • Submit corrected credentialing artifacts with confirmation IDs.
  • Escalate unresolved cases via provider-relations channels and document call references.

3. Refile and monitor

  • Rebill held claims after payer file confirmation.
  • Audit first remits for payment accuracy and unexpected edits.

4. Add prevention controls

  • Promote fixes into standing SOPs.
  • Add re-attestation and re-credentialing deadlines to recurring compliance calendar alerts.

KPIs to Track Monthly

  • Enrollment-related denial/hold rate
  • Average days from credentialing notice to completion
  • Claims impacted by provider-eligibility edits
  • Appeal overturn rate for enrollment-related denials

Final Takeaway

Treat CAQH re-attestation and payer recredentialing as documented revenue-cycle operations rather than occasional administrative cleanup. The controls above are intended to surface missing items before they become larger enrollment or claim-workflow problems.
