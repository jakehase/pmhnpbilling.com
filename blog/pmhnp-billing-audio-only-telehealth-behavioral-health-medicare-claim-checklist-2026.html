PMHNP Billing: Audio-Only Telehealth for Behavioral Health, Medicare Rules, and Claim QA Checklist (2026)

A practical PMHNP telehealth guide for practices that want cleaner audio-only claim decisions, fewer field-level mistakes, and less preventable revenue leakage when video is not the real visit path.

By PMHNP Billing editorial team · Published July 25, 2026 · 11 min read · Updated July 25, 2026

Audio-only telehealth is one of the easiest PMHNP billing workflows to oversimplify. A team hears that behavioral telehealth can be done by phone, turns on a few remote-visit fields, and assumes the claims should now move cleanly. But the real questions are more specific: which payer product allows audio-only, when does Medicare treat behavioral telehealth differently from other telehealth, what does the chart need to prove, and which claim fields should never be copied forward without live verification?

For PMHNP owners, billers, and revenue-cycle leads, audio-only matters because it often shows up in high-friction real life: connectivity failures, patient device limitations, privacy constraints, rural access issues, and follow-up medication-management encounters where video does not actually happen. The operational win is not “bill every phone visit.” It is building a verification-first workflow that separates what Medicare clearly allows from what Medicaid and commercial products may still handle differently.

Primary sources: Telehealth.HHS.gov says Medicare behavioral or mental telehealth can permanently be provided in the patient’s home with no geographic restriction and can permanently be delivered using audio-only communication platforms; see Telehealth policy updates and Medicare payment policies. For commonly used behavioral telehealth code families, review Billing for telebehavioral health. CMS also maintains the current list of Medicare telehealth services.

What Medicare currently allows for PMHNP audio-only behavioral telehealth

The most important starting point is that Medicare behavioral or mental telehealth is not the same as every other telehealth category. Telehealth.HHS.gov says Medicare patients can permanently receive behavioral or mental telehealth in the home, there are no geographic restrictions for that category, and behavioral or mental telehealth in Medicare can permanently be delivered using audio-only communication platforms.

Medicare rule area What it means for PMHNP billing Operational takeaway
Behavioral or mental telehealth in the home Home-based behavioral telehealth is permanently allowed in Medicare. Do not assume the patient must travel to an originating site for Medicare behavioral claims.
No geographic restriction for behavioral or mental telehealth Rural-only assumptions do not control Medicare behavioral telehealth access. Train front-end staff not to deny scheduling or billing just because the patient is outside a traditional rural telehealth setup.
Audio-only allowed permanently for behavioral or mental telehealth Phone-based behavioral telehealth may be billable when the service and payer rules align. Focus on claim and documentation accuracy instead of treating audio-only as automatically noncovered.
In-person requirement waived through December 31, 2027 The six-month initial and annual in-person follow-up requirement is not currently required through that date. Track the policy horizon, but do not build 2026 Medicare workflow around a requirement that is currently waived.
Interactive telecommunications system standard Telehealth.HHS.gov says audio-only may count when the practitioner is technically capable of interactive telehealth but the patient is not capable of, or does not consent to, video technology. Capture the modality reality in the note instead of leaving staff to guess later why the visit was audio-only.
Important boundary: Medicare behavioral telehealth rules do not automatically prove that a commercial plan, Medicare Advantage product, or state Medicaid program will want the same modifier, place of service, documentation language, or code recognition. Audio-only is one of the worst places to universalize payer assumptions.

Why audio-only PMHNP claims still deny even when the visit was legitimate

The clinical encounter may be real and appropriate, but the claim can still fail because the revenue-cycle team collapsed too many decisions into one step. Common breakdowns include using the wrong payer rule set, copying video-visit fields onto an audio-only claim, failing to document the modality clearly, or assuming every phone-based psychiatric encounter belongs on the same coding path.

Failure pattern What goes wrong Safer next step
Video rules copied onto an audio-only encounter The claim carries telehealth fields that do not match the actual modality. Separate audio-only from video workflows in the charge-review SOP.
Medicare rules applied to every payer Commercial or managed-care claims deny because the product has different field or coverage expectations. Maintain a payer-by-product audio-only matrix rather than one global telehealth rule.
Modality not stated clearly in the chart Billers cannot prove whether the visit was video, audio-only, or a failed hybrid attempt. Make modality a required field in every PMHNP remote note template.
Psychotherapy and E/M coding path not reviewed carefully The underlying psychiatric code selection is weak before the telehealth layer is even evaluated. Pair telehealth QA with the PMHNP psychiatric coding guide.
First-wave denials treated as random The same field error or payer mismatch repeats across multiple claims. Tag every audio-only denial by product, code family, and exact payer language.

The audio-only decision screen PMHNP teams should use before claim release

A useful workflow begins before billing. The team should decide what happened, what the payer allows, and which claim path fits the actual encounter.

  • Was the encounter truly audio-only, or did video occur for part of the visit?
  • Which payer and exact product controls the claim?
  • Is the underlying psychiatric code on that payer’s telehealth-eligible list?
  • For Medicare, does the note show the patient was at home and the encounter was behavioral or mental telehealth?
  • When payer policy makes it relevant, does the note explain why audio-only was used rather than video?
  • Did the visit include E/M work, psychotherapy, crisis services, family therapy, or another path that changes the coding review?
  • Can the team prove the current modifier and place-of-service fields from an official payer source or live payer guidance?
Fast rule: the phrase “phone visit” is not enough for billing. The practice needs a verified payer rule, a supported psychiatric code path, and documentation that makes the audio-only modality unmistakable.

Common PMHNP code families worth double-checking

Telehealth.HHS.gov’s telebehavioral billing guide lists commonly used behavioral telehealth codes that include psychiatric diagnostic evaluations, psychotherapy, crisis psychotherapy, family psychotherapy, and established outpatient visits. For PMHNP practices, that means the telehealth question usually sits on top of a familiar psychiatric coding decision rather than replacing it.

Code family Why PMHNP practices use it Audio-only QA question
90791 / 90792 Diagnostic evaluations, including with medical services when appropriate. Does the payer allow this service remotely under the actual modality used?
90833 / 90836 / 90838 Psychotherapy add-ons paired with medical evaluation work. Was the underlying E/M plus psychotherapy structure documented clearly enough before telehealth fields were added?
90834 / 90837 Psychotherapy visit paths that may appear in psychiatric practices depending on service model. Did the payer recognize this telehealth path for audio-only, not just video?
99212-99215 Established outpatient E/M visits that often anchor medication-management follow-ups. Does the note support the E/M level and the payer’s current telehealth field logic for audio-only?
90839 / 90840 and other special paths Crisis or unusual visit types can create payer-specific exceptions or denials. Has someone verified this exact code family instead of assuming the standard follow-up rule applies?

Do not universalize modifier 93, POS 02, POS 10, or legacy telehealth fields

Audio-only claims fail when staff turn one payer’s field pattern into a universal rule. Some products may use modifier 93 for synchronous audio-only services. Others may use different telehealth logic, legacy modifiers, or product-specific instructions. Some systems key off place of service in ways that differ from others. A PMHNP practice should treat audio-only field selection as a payer-product configuration question, not a memory test.

Field area Why it is risky Control to add
Modifier selection Teams often assume one audio-only modifier fits every payer. Keep the exact payer source for each modifier expectation in the matrix.
Place of service The patient’s actual location and the payer’s telehealth rules may not be reflected correctly. Link POS review to the existing POS 02 vs 10 telehealth checklist.
Underlying code eligibility Even a correct modifier does not rescue a code the payer does not accept that way. Verify the code family first, then the telehealth fields.
Documentation prompts Billing teams cannot defend a claim when the note barely mentions modality. Require the note to state audio-only, patient location, and any payer-relevant reason video was not used.
Product drift over time Telehealth policies change more often than many front-end SOPs do. Date-stamp each payer rule and recheck on a scheduled cadence.

Same-day claim QA checklist for PMHNP audio-only visits

  1. Confirm the encounter was actually audio-only and that the chart says so plainly.
  2. Verify the responsible payer and exact product before any telehealth fields are applied.
  3. Review whether the underlying psychiatric code is appropriate on its own merits.
  4. Check the payer’s current telehealth rule for audio-only modality, including code recognition and field expectations.
  5. Confirm the note includes patient location, service modality, and any payer-relevant video limitation or non-consent fact when required.
  6. For Medicare behavioral telehealth, make sure the workflow reflects the permanent home-based and audio-only policy rather than outdated originating-site assumptions.
  7. Tag the claim for early post-adjudication review so any denial becomes a matrix update, not a repeated surprise.
Do not change the visit story to make the claim fit. If the encounter was audio-only, document and bill from that truth. The fix for a weak payer setup is better routing and payer verification—not rewriting the modality after the fact.

FAQ: audio-only telehealth for PMHNP billing

Can PMHNPs bill Medicare for audio-only behavioral telehealth?

Yes, Medicare behavioral and mental telehealth can be furnished in the patient’s home with no geographic restriction, and Telehealth.HHS.gov says behavioral or mental telehealth services in Medicare can permanently be delivered using audio-only communication platforms.

Does audio-only telehealth mean every payer wants modifier 93?

No. Audio-only billing fields vary by payer, product, and system configuration. A PMHNP practice should verify each payer’s modifier, place-of-service, and documentation expectations instead of assuming one national field pattern fits every claim.

What documentation matters most on an audio-only PMHNP claim?

The chart should clearly show the service modality, why audio-only was used when payer rules make that relevant, the patient’s location, the services actually performed, and enough clinical support for the underlying psychiatric code.

When should a PMHNP practice stop and verify before releasing an audio-only claim?

Stop and verify when the payer product is new, the visit type is uncommon, telehealth fields changed recently, the visit mixed psychotherapy and E/M work, or the team cannot prove the payer’s current audio-only rules from an official source or live payer guidance.

Final word

Audio-only PMHNP billing works best when the practice stops treating telehealth as one giant category. Medicare behavioral telehealth has a clearer permanent home-based and audio-only path than many teams realize, but that does not remove the need for code-level review, product-level verification, and disciplined charting. When those pieces are explicit, audio-only visits stop feeling like edge cases and start behaving like controlled revenue-cycle work.
