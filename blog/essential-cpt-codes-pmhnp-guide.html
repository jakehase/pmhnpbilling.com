PMHNP Psychiatric Billing Code Families: A Verification-First 2026 Guide

A practical overview of common psychiatric evaluation, E/M, psychotherapy, crisis, and telehealth code paths—without unsupported rate promises.

By PMHNP Billing editorial team · Published February 1, 2026 · Updated July 9, 2026

PMHNP coding decisions should begin with the service actually performed and documented—not with a desired payment amount. The same diagnosis or appointment length can support different code paths depending on medical decision making, separately documented psychotherapy, time rules, setting, payer policy, and the current code set.

Important: This page is educational and does not replace the current CPT code set, payer manuals, coding advice, or a qualified review of the record. CPT content and payer edits change. Verify the code, descriptor, time rule, bundling rule, and coverage policy in effect for the date of service.

Psychiatric diagnostic evaluation: 90791 and 90792

The 90791/90792 family is used for psychiatric diagnostic evaluation. The distinction is not simply “new patient” versus “established patient,” and prescribing alone should not be used as a shortcut. Review the current code definitions, the components of the service, provider scope, and payer credentialing rules before selecting the path.

  • Document the diagnostic assessment and clinically relevant history.
  • Make the record support the medical services, if any, represented by the selected code.
  • Check payer limits on frequency, same-day combinations, and provider type.

Office and outpatient E/M: 99202–99205 and 99211–99215

Office/outpatient E/M selection is generally based on medical decision making or total time when the current rules permit time-based selection. A diagnosis, prescription, or long appointment does not automatically establish a higher level.

For a defensible E/M workflow, document the problems addressed, data reviewed or analyzed when applicable, and management risk. If selecting by time, capture the qualifying total time and the work included under the current rule. Do not count time that is separately reported.

Standalone psychotherapy: 90832, 90834, and 90837

These psychotherapy code families represent different time bands. Use the exact current CPT time thresholds rather than rounded appointment labels. The note should identify the psychotherapy service, therapeutic approach or interventions, patient response, and connection to the treatment plan.

Do not assume a psychotherapy code is supported simply because counseling occurred during medication management. The record must support a separately reportable psychotherapy service when one is billed.

Psychotherapy add-ons with E/M: 90833, 90836, and 90838

These add-on codes may be used with an eligible E/M service when the requirements for both services are met. Keep the psychotherapy time and work distinct from the E/M work, and verify the payer accepts the combination for that provider and setting.

  • Select the E/M level from its own criteria.
  • Document the separately identifiable psychotherapy and qualifying time.
  • Check payer edits, telehealth rules, and same-day restrictions.

Interactive complexity: 90785

Interactive complexity is not a general “difficult visit” add-on. It has specific communication-related criteria and eligible base services. The note should identify the qualifying circumstance and how it complicated delivery of the service. Routine use without specific support creates audit risk.

Crisis psychotherapy: 90839 and 90840

Crisis psychotherapy has its own clinical and time requirements. A same-day urgent appointment is not automatically crisis psychotherapy. Confirm that the encounter meets the current definition, document the crisis-focused assessment and intervention, and apply the exact time rule.

Telehealth is a second layer—not a separate coding shortcut

Start with the covered service, then apply the payer's telehealth requirements. Place of service, modifier, eligible provider, patient location, modality, and originating-site rules can differ by payer and product. POS 02 and POS 10 have different patient-location meanings in the Medicare framework, but a commercial or Medicaid plan may publish additional instructions.

Do not copy one payer's modifier or POS configuration across the entire payer matrix. Save the policy source and effective date with each rule.

Do not use a generic reimbursement table as a fee schedule

Allowed amounts vary by payer, plan, provider contract, geography, facility status, modifier, network status, and date of service. A public estimate cannot establish what a particular claim should pay. For Medicare, use the official Physician Fee Schedule lookup. For contracted plans, compare the remittance with the applicable contract or documented rate source.

Pre-bill code verification checklist

  1. Confirm the finalized note reflects the service performed.
  2. Verify the current code and time rule for the date of service.
  3. Separate E/M, psychotherapy, and add-on work where required.
  4. Confirm provider type, setting, and payer coverage.
  5. Apply telehealth POS and modifiers from the payer/product source.
  6. Run bundling and same-day edits before submission.
  7. Escalate uncertain cases to a qualified coding resource.
