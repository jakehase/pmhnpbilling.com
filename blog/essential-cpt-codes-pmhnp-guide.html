Essential CPT Codes for PMHNPs: The Complete 2026 Billing Guide

Understanding which CPT codes to use—and when—can mean the difference between optimal reimbursement and leaving thousands of dollars on the table. This comprehensive guide breaks down the essential billing codes every psychiatric mental health nurse practitioner needs to master.

Published February 1, 2026

Every day, Chicago-area PMHNPs provide essential mental health services but struggle with proper coding. Using the wrong CPT code—or missing opportunities to bill for additional services—directly impacts your bottom line. One local practice I worked with was undercoding by an average of $75 per visit simply because they weren't familiar with the full range of available codes.

This guide covers the CPT codes you'll use most frequently in psychiatric practice, with practical examples, documentation requirements, and 2026 reimbursement rates for the Chicago market.

Psychiatric Diagnostic Evaluation Codes

Initial patient encounters require specific evaluation codes that establish the foundation for ongoing care. Using the correct code ensures proper reimbursement for the comprehensive assessment work PMHNPs perform.

CPT Code Description Typical Reimbursement*
90791 Psychiatric diagnostic evaluation (without medical services) $180–$240
90792 Psychiatric diagnostic evaluation with medical services $220–$295

*Approximate Medicare rates for Illinois; commercial insurers may vary significantly

When to Use 90791 vs 90792

The distinction is straightforward but critical: 90792 includes medical services—specifically, the prescription of psychotropic medications. If you perform a comprehensive diagnostic evaluation and prescribe medication during that initial visit, bill 90792. If you're conducting the evaluation without prescribing (perhaps deferring medication decisions to a subsequent visit or the patient is already stable on existing medications), use 90791.

Billing Tip: Some payers restrict 90792 to MDs/DOs, though PMHNPs with independent prescribing authority should qualify. Verify your specific payer contracts, and if denied, appeal with documentation of your prescriptive authority and scope of practice.

Psychotherapy Codes: The Bread and Butter of PMHNP Practice

Most established patient visits involve psychotherapy combined with medication management. PMHNPs have several code options depending on visit duration and complexity.

CPT Code Description Duration Typical Reimbursement*
90832 Psychotherapy, 30 minutes 16–37 min $75–$105
90834 Psychotherapy, 45 minutes 38–52 min $105–$145
90837 Psychotherapy, 60 minutes 53+ min $140–$185

*Approximate Medicare rates for Illinois

Add-On Codes for Complex Cases

When psychotherapy sessions involve complex factors requiring additional time and clinical judgment, add-on codes increase your reimbursement appropriately:

CPT Code Description Requirements Add-on Rate*
+90785 Interactive complexity Complex communication factors, maladaptive communication, emotions/behavior, mandated reporting $18–$25
+99354 Prolonged services (first hour) 30–74 minutes beyond typical time $85–$120
Documentation Requirement for 90785: Your note must specifically identify which interactive complexity factors were present and how they impacted the delivery of care. Generic statements like "session was complex" will not support the add-on code.

Medication Management and E/M Codes

For established patients where the primary focus is medication management rather than psychotherapy, PMHNPs should use Evaluation and Management (E/M) codes. The 2021 E/M coding changes simplified this process significantly.

CPT Code Description Medical Decision Making Typical Reimbursement*
99213 Office/outpatient visit, established patient Low complexity $95–$125
99214 Office/outpatient visit, established patient Moderate complexity $135–$180
99215 Office/outpatient visit, established patient High complexity $185–$245

The Critical Distinction: Psychotherapy vs. E/M

This is where many PMHNPs lose revenue. If you spend significant time providing psychotherapy and manage medications, you have billing options:

Option 1: Bill the psychotherapy code (90834, 90837) when therapy is the primary service and document that you addressed medications.

Option 2: Bill an E/M code (99213-99215) when medication management dominates and psychotherapy is incidental.

Option 3: In some cases, both a psychotherapy code AND an E/M code may be appropriate for the same visit, though many payers won't reimburse both at 100%. Check your specific contracts.

Common Error: Billing 99213 for a 45-minute visit where you provided substantial psychotherapy. If therapy dominated the session, use 90834 instead—you'll typically earn more and the coding more accurately reflects the service provided.

Crisis Intervention and Urgent Care Codes

PMHNPs managing crisis situations have specific codes that reflect the intensity and complexity of these encounters.

CPT Code Description Use Case Typical Reimbursement*
90839 Psychotherapy for crisis, first 60 minutes Initial crisis session $180–$240
+90840 Psychotherapy for crisis, each additional 30 minutes Crisis sessions exceeding 60 minutes $75–$105

Crisis codes require a true psychiatric crisis—suicidal ideation, severe decompensation, acute psychosis, or similar emergency presentations. Routine urgent appointments don't qualify. Document the specific crisis factors present and the interventions required.

Group Psychotherapy and Family Sessions

Many PMHNPs overlook these revenue opportunities. Group and family services often reimburse well and can improve patient outcomes.

CPT Code Description Duration Typical Reimbursement*
90853 Group psychotherapy Typically 45–60 min $35–$55 per patient
90846 Family psychotherapy (without patient) 50 minutes $95–$130
90847 Family psychotherapy (with patient) 50 minutes $105–$145

Revenue Maximization Strategy: A group session with 8 patients at $45 each generates $360 for a single hour—often more profitable than individual sessions, with lower documentation burden per patient.

Telehealth Billing: Essential Modifiers

With telehealth now standard in psychiatric practice, understanding the correct modifiers prevents claim denials.

Modifier Description When to Use
-95 Synchronous telemedicine service Most commercial payers for real-time video visits
-GT Via interactive audio and video telecommunication systems Medicare and some Medicaid plans
-GY Item or service statutorily excluded When telehealth isn't covered but you want denial for patient responsibility

Case Study: Optimizing a Typical PMHNP Schedule

Consider a Chicago PMHNP seeing 20 patients weekly. Before code optimization, they billed primarily 99213 for established patients ($110 average) and 90791 for new evaluations ($210), generating approximately:

  • 16 established visits × $110 = $1,760
  • 4 new evaluations × $210 = $840
  • Weekly total: $2,600

After implementing proper coding based on actual services provided:

  • 10 medication management visits × $125 (99213/99214 mix) = $1,250
  • 6 psychotherapy visits × $145 (90834) = $870
  • 2 complex visits with 90785 add-on × $165 = $330
  • 4 new evaluations × $260 (90792) = $1,040
  • Weekly total: $3,490

Annual revenue increase: $46,280—simply from accurate coding.

Documentation Requirements That Protect Your Revenue

Proper coding means nothing without supporting documentation. Illinois payers increasingly require specific elements:

For Psychotherapy Codes (90832-90837): Document the specific therapeutic interventions used, patient's response, and how the session addressed treatment goals. Time must be clearly documented.

For E/M Codes (99213-99215): Focus on medical decision-making elements: problems addressed, data reviewed, and risk. The 2021 changes mean you no longer need to document specific exam elements for most psychiatric visits.

For Add-On Codes: Specifically describe the factors justifying the additional complexity. Interactive complexity requires identifying the communication barriers or circumstances that complicated care delivery.

Common Coding Mistakes to Avoid

Undercoding Due to Time Misunderstandings: Many PMHNPs bill 99213 for 30-minute visits when 90832 (psychotherapy) or 99214 (moderate complexity E/M) would be more appropriate and profitable.

Overlooking Crisis Codes: True crisis interventions should be coded as 90839/90840, not regular psychotherapy codes. The reimbursement difference is substantial.

Incorrect Use of 90785: This add-on code requires specific interactive complexity factors. Using it routinely will trigger audits.

Missing Family Session Opportunities: When family members participate in sessions, 90846 or 90847 often applies and reimburses better than individual therapy codes.

2026 Updates and Changes

Several coding changes affect PMHNPs in 2026:

Prolonged Services Coding: New prolonged services codes (99417, G2212) offer additional reimbursement for visits significantly exceeding typical timeframes, but require careful documentation of medical necessity.

Telehealth Place of Service: Illinois now requires POS 10 (telehealth in patient's home) or POS 02 (telehealth in other location) rather than POS 11 with telehealth modifiers. Using the wrong POS code causes automatic denials.

Behavioral Health Integration: Collaborative care codes (99492-99494) now have expanded coverage in Illinois, creating opportunities for PMHNPs working with primary care practices.

Your Action Plan

Accurate CPT coding is a learned skill that directly impacts your practice's financial health. Start by auditing your recent claims: Are you consistently using the most appropriate codes for services provided? Are you capturing add-on code opportunities?

Consider investing in specialized billing training or partnering with a psychiatric billing specialist. The revenue increase from proper coding typically far exceeds the cost of expert guidance.

Remember: these codes represent the valuable services you provide patients. Accurate coding ensures you receive appropriate compensation while maintaining the documentation standards that protect your practice during audits.
