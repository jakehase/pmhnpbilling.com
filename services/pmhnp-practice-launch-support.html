The operating goal

A practice that can identify coverage, submit correctly, receive remits, and reconcile payment.

A website, scheduler, NPI, EHR login, or credentialing approval can each be necessary without making the revenue cycle operational. Launch readiness means the facts and systems agree from scheduling through adjudication.

Before the visit

Payer status, eligibility, benefits, authorization/referral rules, estimate and consent workflows, and patient communication are defined.

From encounter to claim

Provider-approved documentation and codes move through configured entity, provider, location, payer, and clearinghouse paths.

From remit to ledger

ERA/EOB and EFT/check activity reconcile to the claim and bank path, with denials and variances entering a visible queue.

Launch sequence

Six connected workstreams, completed in the right order.

  1. Practice facts: confirm legal entity, ownership, tax identity, individual/group NPIs, taxonomy, service locations, licenses, malpractice, W-9, banking, and intended billing structure.
  2. Payer path: define target payer/products, align CAQH, submit necessary applications, and track credentialing, contracting, enrollment/loading, group affiliation, and effective date separately.
  3. Systems: translate requirements into the selected EHR, practice-management system, clearinghouse, payer IDs, portal access, EDI, ERA, and EFT setup.
  4. Front-end controls: set ordinary eligibility, benefits, authorization/referral, demographic, insurance, estimate, and patient-responsibility workflows appropriate to the practice.
  5. Claim controls: configure provider, location, payer, service, telehealth, and claim defaults; test representative paths without inventing clinical code or diagnosis choices.
  6. First-claim reconciliation: follow representative claims through acceptance, adjudication, remit, payment routing, posting, and any exception that exposes a configuration gap.

Ownership boundary

I own billing operations; the provider owns clinical decisions.

PMHNP Billing can own

  • Enrollment operations, status follow-up, and readiness tracking.
  • Billing-system and clearinghouse setup within the agreed scope.
  • Claims, rejections, posting, payer follow-up, denial work, and reporting after launch.
  • Coordination among payer, EHR, clearinghouse, and practice workflows.

Provider or practice owns

  • Legal/entity decisions and authoritative ownership, tax, license, banking, and disclosure facts.
  • Contracts, signatures, attestations, and secure access approvals.
  • Clinical documentation and selection or approval of diagnoses and service codes.
  • Clinical, scheduling, patient-consent, and financial-policy decisions.

Payers control

  • Network availability, credentialing and contracting decisions.
  • Enrollment loading, product participation, and effective dates.
  • Coverage, medical-necessity, adjudication, recoupment, timing, and payment decisions.

Timing without guarantees

Plan backward from opening, but gate insured billing on verified status.

Start the operational work as early as practical once the practice's entity, provider, location, and target-payer facts are stable. Different workstreams can run in parallel, but a calendar date does not override payer status or missing system configuration.

Do not collapse these dates

Planned opening, application submission, credentialing approval, contract execution, enrollment/load, network effective date, first claim, first remit, and first payment are different dates. The launch plan should show which one is actually proven.

Commercial fit

Scope launch work around the actual practice.

Launch and credentialing project scope depends on entity structure, provider/location count, target payers, current status, selected systems, and whether ongoing billing is included. The first review maps those variables before a project scope is proposed.

When ongoing full-service billing is a fit, pricing is 5% of collections, with no setup fee and no monthly minimum. Stand-alone credentialing or launch projects are scoped separately.

Frequently asked questions

What a launch review can and cannot promise.

Can I start while I am still choosing an EHR?

Yes. Entity, identifier, payer, workflow, and requirements mapping can start while systems are being evaluated. Final configuration waits for the selected platform and verified practice facts.

Will you decide which insurers I should join?

I can organize local and practice-specific factors, payer products, administrative burden, and operational dependencies. The practice makes the business decision; payer panel availability and participation decisions remain payer-controlled.

Can you guarantee I will be in network by opening day?

No. The plan can reduce preventable delay and keep follow-up active, but payer review, panel status, contracting, loading, and effective dates cannot be guaranteed.

What should I send through the contact form?

A patient-free summary of state, solo/group structure, target opening date, expected payer mix, EHR status, and what is already complete. Do not send credentials, banking records, tax documents, patient data, or PHI through the public form.
