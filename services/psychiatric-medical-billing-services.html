What are psychiatric billing services?

Psychiatric billing services manage the financial path from insurance and claim readiness through adjudication, payment posting, denial work, and aging A/R. Psychiatry billing differs from generic claim submission because medical and behavioral-health rules often overlap in the same patient journey.

For PMHNP practices, the workflow may include psychiatric diagnostic evaluations, medication-management E/M visits, psychotherapy add-ons, telehealth place-of-service and modifier rules, prior authorizations, behavioral-health carve-outs, and payer-specific provider enrollment. Each claim needs a documented status, next action, and owner instead of disappearing into a month-end aging report.

Psychiatry billing controls built around real claim risks

  • Diagnostic and follow-up coding: claim-readiness review for 90791/90792, outpatient E/M, and psychotherapy add-on workflows. See the 90791 vs 90792 PMHNP billing guide.
  • Telehealth: payer-product checks for place of service, modifiers, modality, patient location, and effective dates. Use the telehealth billing support overview.
  • Authorizations: approval, units or visits, expiration, renewal, and claim-to-authorization matching. Review prior authorization support.
  • Enrollment and routing: NPI, taxonomy, group/individual participation, CAQH, reassignment, carve-out, ERA, and EFT dependencies.
  • Denials and A/R: rejection versus denial classification, timely correction, payer follow-up, appeal-ready records, and recurring root-cause reporting.

A practical psychiatric revenue-cycle workflow

1. Make the claim ready before submission

Confirm demographics, eligibility, payer routing, rendering and billing provider setup, authorization status, service details, and known payer edits before release.

2. Track the first payer response

Separate accepted claims from clearinghouse rejections and payer-level failures quickly. A rejected claim is not safely "in process" just because it left the EHR.

3. Post and reconcile the remittance

Post ERA/EOB results, identify contractual adjustments, patient responsibility, takebacks, and possible underpayments, and preserve the remittance trail for follow-up.

4. Work denials and aging A/R by next action

Classify the issue, assign the correction or payer call, preserve timely-filing evidence, and feed repeat problems back into scheduling, enrollment, authorization, or documentation workflows.

5. Report what should change upstream

Useful mental health RCM reporting shows clean-claim rate, denial mix, aging by payer, unresolved enrollment or authorization dependencies, underpayments, and repeated preventable errors—not just a total balance.
