Milestone clarity

“Approved” is not the last operational checkpoint.

  1. Application received: the payer confirms intake, but no participation decision is implied.
  2. Credentialing approved: the provider passes the payer's credentialing review.
  3. Contracting completed: applicable participation terms are executed.
  4. Enrollment or roster loaded: the payer's claims system recognizes the provider, entity, location, and required affiliations.
  5. Effective date confirmed: the date and product/network applicability are documented.
  6. EDI, ERA, and EFT active: claim and remit/payment routing are configured.
  7. First claim reconciled: a representative claim is accepted, adjudicated, and matched to the expected remit/payment path.

Payer-controlled timing, panel availability, approval, contracting, effective dates, claim adjudication, and payment cannot be guaranteed.

Operational scope

What I handle payer by payer.

Profile and identifier alignment

  • CAQH profile, documents, and reattestation tracking.
  • Individual/group NPI, taxonomy, W-9, address, ownership, license, and malpractice alignment.
  • Identification of conflicting records before they cause payer rework.

Applications and follow-up

  • Prepare and track payer applications and requested attachments.
  • Respond to operational follow-up and document payer reference numbers.
  • Maintain a status matrix with the actual next action and owner.

Contract and effective-date checks

  • Track contracting and enrollment/loading separately from credentialing approval.
  • Confirm effective date, products/networks, service location, group affiliation, and directory state where available.
  • Escalate discrepancies instead of assuming approval equals claim readiness.

Billing handoff

  • Coordinate payer IDs, EDI enrollment, clearinghouse routing, ERA, and EFT.
  • Validate eligibility and claim-routing prerequisites.
  • Carry the milestone into first-claim and first-remit reconciliation when billing is in scope.

Clear responsibilities

I run the process; the practice supplies authoritative facts and signatures.

PMHNP Billing owns

  • Application preparation and operational submission workflow.
  • Document/status tracking and payer follow-up.
  • Milestone reconciliation, discrepancy escalation, and billing handoff.

Working sequence

A status review before more applications.

  1. Inventory: providers, entities, locations, target payers, existing applications, approvals, contracts, and planned opening dates.
  2. Normalize: reconcile CAQH, NPI/NPPES, W-9, taxonomy, licenses, addresses, and ownership facts.
  3. Submit and track: manage only the required payer paths and document payer references, requests, and next actions.
  4. Validate readiness: confirm effective date, affiliations, EDI, ERA/EFT, and representative claim routing before treating the payer as operationally live.

Frequently asked questions

Credentialing without false certainty.

Can you guarantee a payer acceptance date?

No. Payers control panel availability, review, contracting, roster cycles, enrollment loading, effective dates, and requests for additional information. I can keep the process complete, tracked, and actively followed up.

Should I schedule insured patients as soon as an approval email arrives?

Not based on that email alone. Confirm the applicable network/product, group and location affiliation, enrollment/load status, effective date, and claim-routing readiness. Financial and patient-notice decisions should match the verified status.

Do I need a Type 2 NPI?

That depends on the legal/entity and billing structure. The right setup is validated against the practice's actual ownership, contracting, payment, and claim configuration rather than assumed from practice size alone.

Can this continue into full-service billing?

Yes. Credentialing and billing can be coordinated so payer status, EDI/ERA/EFT, eligibility, authorization, claims, and remits remain connected. Full-service billing is 5% of collections with no setup fee or monthly minimum.
