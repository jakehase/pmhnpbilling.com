What I own

The recurring billing loop, not just claim transmission.

Claims and rejections

  • Prepare and submit claims from provider-approved encounters and established payer/location rules.
  • Review clearinghouse edits and correct operational data issues.
  • Track accepted claims into payer processing rather than stopping at submission.

Posting and reconciliation

  • Post ERA/EOB payments, adjustments, and patient responsibility.
  • Reconcile remits, deposits, takebacks, and unexplained variances.
  • Surface missing remits or payment-routing problems for resolution.

Payer follow-up and denials

  • Work aging claims and payer status exceptions.
  • Identify denial cause and choose the correct operational path: correction, reconsideration, appeal, or provider clarification.
  • Track action and outcome instead of repeatedly touching the same balance.

Useful reporting

  • Show claim, payment, denial, and A/R status in practical terms.
  • Separate routine work from exceptions that need a practice decision.
  • Call out recurring root causes worth fixing in configuration or workflow.

Real-biller workflow

Established setup is trusted; exceptions trigger deeper review.

One-time setup and validation

  • Map entities, providers, locations, payers, EHR, clearinghouse, ERA/EFT, and current A/R ownership.
  • Validate representative claim and remit paths.
  • Confirm transition dates and who owns pre-transition balances.

Routine clean claims

For an established provider, payer, location, and code configuration, I rely on the signed/provider-approved encounter, configured defaults, ordinary scrubbing, and visible-field checks. A clean claim does not need a complete enrollment and documentation audit every time.

Exception review

I go deeper when something is new or changed, a required field is missing, facts visibly conflict, eligibility or authorization is genuinely in question, the scrubber rejects the claim, the payer denies, or a planned sample audit calls for it.

Onboarding

A controlled handoff with explicit ownership.

  1. Fit review: confirm practice stage, payer mix, EHR, volume, current pain points, and the requested scope.
  2. Access and inventory: use secure access paths to map configuration, open claims, remits, denials, A/R, and payer portals.
  3. Launch checks: verify representative claim/remit routing and agree on cutover dates and escalation rules.
  4. Recurring operations: run claims, posting, follow-up, denial work, reconciliation, and reporting with a visible exception queue.

Clinical boundary

The rendering provider selects or approves diagnoses and service codes supported by the signed encounter. I do not invent clinical facts or select unsupported levels; I own the agreed billing operations around the provider-approved record and escalate actual conflicts.

Frequently asked questions

Before you switch or start.

Is this only for Illinois practices?

No. PMHNP Billing supports psychiatric practices nationwide. Payer, state, telehealth, Medicaid, and enrollment rules are validated for the practice's actual footprint.

Do you guarantee payer payment or collections?

No. I manage the agreed billing work and follow-up, but payer coverage, adjudication, timing, recoupment, and payment decisions remain payer-controlled.

Can you take over an existing A/R?

Yes, when the inventory and ownership are clear. We define which balances are in scope, preserve filing-limit evidence, and avoid duplicate work with the outgoing biller.

What should I send through the website form?

Only a short, non-patient-specific description of the practice and billing need. Do not send patient names, claim screenshots, portal credentials, or PHI through the public form.
