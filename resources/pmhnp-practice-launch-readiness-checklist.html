Ungated working checklist

PMHNP practice launch readiness checklist.

Use this worksheet to separate what is verified, pending, payer-controlled, and actually blocking the billing path. It is designed for a solo or growing psychiatric practice preparing to see insured patients.

Start the checklist

How to use it

Record evidence and the next action—not just a checkmark.

For each item, mark one status: verified, in progress, payer/vendor controlled, blocked, or not applicable. Add the evidence, owner, and next action in your private operations record. Do not write credentials, banking data, patient information, PHI, or claim details on a public or shared copy.

Verified

There is current evidence for the actual provider/entity/location/payer combination—not an assumption or a status for a different setup.

Controlled but pending

The payer or vendor owns the next decision or processing step, and the follow-up date/reference is documented.

Blocking

A missing or conflicting fact prevents the next safe step. Name the owner and resolve it before treating that pathway as ready.

Gate 1

Practice, provider, and location facts

  • □ Legal entity name, ownership, tax classification, EIN, and W-9 agree.
  • □ Individual NPI and—where the billing structure requires it—organization NPI are active and accurate.
  • □ Primary and secondary taxonomy choices reflect the provider and intended services.
  • □ Service, billing, mailing, and pay-to addresses are intentionally mapped.
  • □ State licenses, controlled-substance registrations where applicable, and malpractice coverage are current for the intended footprint.
  • □ Group/individual claim and payment structure is defined rather than inferred from practice size.

Gate 2

Payer enrollment and network status

  • □ Target payers and specific products/networks are listed by priority.
  • □ CAQH profile facts and documents match NPI, W-9, licenses, malpractice, addresses, ownership, and work history.
  • □ Each payer path has an application/reference number, current milestone, last contact, next follow-up, and owner.
  • □ Credentialing approval, contract execution, enrollment/roster load, group affiliation, directory status, and effective date are tracked separately.
  • □ Payer/product/location combinations that are not ready are explicitly blocked from being labeled “in network.”
  • □ Recredentialing, reattestation, and expiring-document dates have an owner.

Gate 3

EHR, clearinghouse, portals, EDI, ERA, and EFT

  • □ The selected EHR/practice-management system supports the intended providers, locations, services, telehealth workflows, and reporting needs.
  • □ Billing provider, rendering provider, facility/location, taxonomy, and payer IDs are configured for representative claim paths.
  • □ Clearinghouse enrollment and payer-specific EDI requirements are active where required.
  • □ Payer portal roles follow least-privilege access and have a named account owner.
  • □ ERA delivery is routed to the correct system and remits can be retrieved when an ERA is absent.
  • □ EFT/check payment routing and bank reconciliation ownership are documented without exposing banking credentials in this worksheet.

Gate 4

Scheduling, eligibility, benefits, and authorization controls

  • □ Patient demographic and insurance capture has a visible-field quality check.
  • □ Eligibility and benefits checks document the payer/product, date, source, and material limitations without treating them as a payment guarantee.
  • □ Referral and prior-authorization rules have a defined owner and tracking method where genuinely required.
  • □ Telehealth location/POS and modifier workflows are validated for the actual payer and service.
  • □ Patient estimates, financial policies, consent, and out-of-network communication are aligned with verified payer status.
  • □ Staff know which exceptions require a hold or escalation before the visit/claim.

Gate 5

Documentation, coding, and claim configuration

  • □ The provider understands that diagnoses and service codes must be selected or approved from the signed encounter.
  • □ Documentation completion and charge handoff have an ordinary cutoff and exception path.
  • □ Fee schedules and service lists are configured intentionally without using fabricated or placeholder codes.
  • □ Provider, payer, location, telehealth, and claim defaults are validated once and after material changes.
  • □ Routine clean claims use normal scrubbing and visible-field checks; new/conflicting/missing facts trigger deeper review.
  • □ Corrected claim, reconsideration, appeal, medical-record request, and timely-filing evidence paths are defined.

Gate 6

Representative claim and remit proof

  • □ A representative claim path reaches clearinghouse acceptance and payer acknowledgment.
  • □ Rejections enter an owned correction queue rather than disappearing after transmission.
  • □ Claim status can be followed through adjudication using the appropriate payer/clearinghouse evidence.
  • □ ERA/EOB can be retrieved, posted, and matched to the claim.
  • □ EFT/check activity can be reconciled to the remit and bank path.
  • □ A denial, underpayment, takeback, or missing-remit exception has a visible owner and next action.

Gate 7

Recurring operations after launch

  • □ Claim, posting, reconciliation, A/R, denial, and reporting cadences have named owners.
  • □ Missing encounters, rejections, aging claims, unapplied remits, and recoupments appear in actionable queues.
  • □ Payer, provider, location, EHR, clearinghouse, ownership, and banking changes trigger controlled revalidation.
  • □ Provider questions are limited to real clinical/documentation decisions; normal biller work stays with the billing owner.
  • □ Reporting distinguishes submitted, accepted, adjudicated, paid, denied, adjusted, and reconciled amounts.
  • □ A small planned sample audit supplements—not replaces—the routine clean-claim workflow.

Final launch gate

What must be true before “billing-ready” is accurate?

The actual provider, entity, location, payer/product, EHR, clearinghouse, claim, remit, and payment-routing combination has current evidence; known gaps have owners; payer-controlled items are labeled as such; staff know the front-end workflow; and no planned opening date is being used as a substitute for network or effective-date proof.

This does not guarantee payer approval, coverage, adjudication, processing time, or payment. It establishes a defensible operational starting point and a clear exception path.
