Site standards

Editorial policy

How PMHNP Billing researches, reviews, updates, corrects, and separates educational content from professional advice.

Last updated July 9, 2026

Purpose and audience

PMHNP Billing publishes practical revenue-cycle education for PMHNPs, psychiatric practices, and billing teams. The goal is to make payer and workflow questions easier to investigate and operate—not to replace current payer manuals, contracts, code sets, legal advice, coding advice, or clinical judgment.

Source standards

When a statement depends on a current rule, we prefer primary sources such as CMS, state Medicaid agencies, regulators, payer manuals, official bulletins, and the current code set. Payer and product rules can differ, so readers should verify the source that applies to the member, provider, setting, and date of service.

  • Operational recommendations should be distinguished from binding requirements.
  • Universal deadlines, reimbursement amounts, approval odds, and performance benchmarks are not published without a reliable source and clear scope.
  • Examples should not be presented as real client outcomes unless they are documented and approved for publication.
  • External links are included for verification, not as endorsements.

Authorship and review

Articles are published by the PMHNP Billing editorial team as an organizational author. We do not invent named reviewers, clinical credentials, testimonials, or payer relationships. Drafting and formatting tools may assist the process, but tool output is not treated as an authority; PMHNP Billing remains responsible for the published page.

Updates and corrections

Material edits receive an updated date in the article and structured data. If a payer rule, code reference, or factual statement may be outdated, the safer action is to verify it before relying on it. To report a possible error, use the site contact form and include the page URL, disputed text, and supporting source.

Important boundaries

Site content is educational. It is not legal, medical, tax, compliance, or coding advice and does not establish a client relationship. Clinical decisions remain with the treating clinician. Legal conclusions remain with qualified counsel or the appropriate regulator. Coding and billing decisions must be supported by the record, current rules, and the applicable payer product.
