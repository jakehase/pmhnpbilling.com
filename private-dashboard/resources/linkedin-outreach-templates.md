## 4. Response Templates for Common Objections

### Objection 1: "I'm happy with my current billing solution"
Response:
I'm glad to hear you have a system that works for you! Many providers I work with were satisfied with their current setup too, until we did a quick audit and found opportunities they didn't know existed. My free billing review isn't about fixing something that's broken - it's about optimization. Even good processes can usually be improved, especially with all the recent changes in mental health billing requirements. Would you be open to a 15-minute conversation just to see if there might be any opportunities you're missing? Worst case, you'll confirm you're already doing everything right.

### Objection 2: "I can't afford to switch billing services right now"
Response:
I completely understand - cash flow is crucial, especially for smaller practices. That's actually why I offer the free audit first. Many providers are surprised to learn they're leaving significant money on the table each month. One recent client thought they couldn't afford to make changes, but our audit revealed they were missing about $3,500 monthly in legitimate billable services. The improvements we identified actually paid for themselves within the first month. I'd be happy to do a quick review and show you exactly what we find. If the numbers don't make sense for your situation, no problem at all.

### Objection 3: "I handle my own billing and it works fine"
Response:
That's impressive - billing is complex, and I respect providers who manage it themselves! I'm curious, though, how much time do you spend on billing tasks each week? Most solo providers I talk with spend 5-10 hours weekly on billing-related work. What if you could get that time back to see more patients or simply have a better work-life balance? My clients typically find that outsourcing billing actually increases their revenue (we catch things they miss) while freeing up significant time. Would you be interested in seeing if the numbers work for your practice?

### Objection 4: "I've been burned by billing companies before"
Response:
I'm really sorry to hear that - unfortunately, your experience is all too common. Many billing companies treat psychiatric providers as an afterthought, and the big companies often assign you to someone who doesn't understand mental health billing. That's exactly why I started my practice. I work exclusively with PMHNPs, and when you call, you get me personally - not a rotating cast of account managers. I'm not asking you to commit to anything. Just let me do a free audit and show you exactly what I find. If you don't like what you see, no problem. But I think you'll notice the difference in how I approach psychiatric billing specifically.

### Objection 5: "Now isn't a good time"
Response:
Absolutely - I know how demanding psychiatric practice can be, and I don't want to add to your stress. When might be a better time to connect? I'm happy to put something on the calendar for a few weeks out, or if you prefer, I can follow up in a month or two. In the meantime, if any billing issues come up or you have questions about recent insurance changes, please don't hesitate to reach out. I work with enough PMHNPs that I can usually provide quick guidance even if we're not formally working together yet.

## Usage Guidelines

### Best Practices:
1. **Personalize everything** - Reference their specific location, practice type, or recent achievements
2. **Lead with value** - Always offer the free audit first
3. **Be specific** - Use concrete examples and numbers when possible
4. **Stay professional but warm** - Match the personal tone from pmhnpbilling.com
5. **Follow up appropriately** - Space messages appropriately, don't overwhelm
6. **Know when to back off** - If someone says no clearly, respect that

### Timing Considerations:
- LinkedIn messages: Best sent Tuesday-Thursday, 9-11 AM or 2-4 PM
- Email outreach: Tuesday-Thursday mornings typically perform best
- Follow-up sequences: Space 3-4 days apart, maximum 3 attempts
- Response time: Aim to respond to inquiries within 2-4 hours during business hours

### Customization Tips:
- Research their practice before reaching out
- Reference specific challenges in their area (insurance companies, local regulations)
- Mention any mutual connections or shared experiences
- Adjust formality based on their communication style
- Include local references when appropriate (Chicago neighborhoods, local events, etc.)

Remember: The key is positioning yourself as a helpful resource who understands psychiatric billing, not just another salesperson pushing services.
